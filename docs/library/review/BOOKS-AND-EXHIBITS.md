## Corrections in A Tiny Seed

The original publisher manuscript stays intact in edition.md. The Library record is explicitly labeled as a local adaptation. Its source ISBN identifies the original, not a new publisher edition. Four revised reading pages are recorded with original and new text in books/a-tiny-seed/review-adaptation.json. The cover transcript announces the adaptation. Text and image credits now distinguish the frozen digital-edition designer Karen Lilje from the current publisher-page designer Tarryn-Anne Anderson.

- Page 4: removed the unsupported parental-objection account; Maathai’s 2006 Fresh Air interview directly credits her mother’s agreement and her brother’s question.
- Page 8: replaced statements about all Kenyan people with the rural women’s specific reported needs; the movement’s own history and Maathai’s interview support this scope.
- Page 9: replaced inferred universal happiness and the oversimplified tree-selling account with documented collaborative tree planting and community work.
- Page 10: removed the unspecific assertion that rivers began flowing again and the implication that millions of trees came from one person’s own seeds; retained documented community planting and wider African spread.

Primary biographical checks: [Maathai’s own interview](https://freshairarchive.org/segments/nobel-peace-prize-winner-wangari-maathai), [Green Belt Movement history](https://www.greenbeltmovement.org/history/), [Nobel facts](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/2004/maathai/facts/), [University of Nairobi](https://wmi.uonbi.ac.ke/node/242). Nobel direct browser access returned 403; its indexed primary text supplied dates/award confirmation. The university page timed out directly but its indexed primary text confirms education and teaching. These access limits are not represented as a successful fresh full-page fetch. The remaining personal childhood scenes are the credited publisher author’s biographical storytelling, not newly invented eyewitness claims.

No factual claim about all animals is inferred from fantasy books. The exact published story wording remains, including regional grammar. Story events (a shark siren, a turtle rescue, dreams, fictional royal ants) do not establish current services, animal-care advice or real local history. All corresponding family flags remain.
